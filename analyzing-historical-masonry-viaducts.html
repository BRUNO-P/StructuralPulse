Analyzing Historical Masonry Viaducts: Balancing Heritage and Modern Demands

Author: Bruno Pompei | Published: Aug 2026 | 4 min read

Rail Infrastructure · FEM/FEA · Structural Assessment · Masonry

The Challenge of Legacy Structures

Analyzing existing structures demands extensive knowledge of historical materials and construction methods, especially for century-old infrastructure where original designs no longer exist. Comprehensive structural testing (both destructive and non-destructive), soil investigations, and precise geometrical surveys become critical when documenting these engineering artefacts.

Modern Pressures on Historical Foundations

Existing railway lines increasingly face demands for higher train speeds and axle loads to accommodate sustainable travel initiatives. While economically and environmentally necessary, this evolution places unprecedented strain on aging masonry viaducts. Engineers must now perform safety assessments that balance three competing priorities:

  • Maintenance costs and retrofitting feasibility
  • Residual service life estimation
  • Growing operational load demands

All of this must be achieved while preserving absolute structural safety and heritage value.

The Complexity of Masonry Assessment

Quantifying "acceptable safety" is exceptionally challenging for masonry structures. Their behavior depends on crack propagation, load redistribution through arches, and material heterogeneity—factors poorly captured by conventional linear analysis.

Advanced Modeling: The Smeared Crack Approach

To address this, we employ smeared crack models governed by constitutive laws. This methodology simulates how microcracks alter load paths and force redistribution across arches, revealing potential failure modes. Through incremental analysis with work-controlled steps, we compute fracture energy—a key metric quantifying crack opening displacement and residual capacity. This provides objective safety margins for heritage structures facing 21st-century demands.
